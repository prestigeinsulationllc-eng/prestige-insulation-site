Why Mineral Wool

Preferred when fire, sound, or moisture are a concern.

01

Non-combustible — rated to 2000°F+

Unlike fiberglass, mineral wool does not melt or burn. It's non-combustible and can withstand temperatures above 2,000°F — making it the default choice for fire-rated wall and floor assemblies, apartment corridors, and commercial buildings with strict fire code requirements.

02

Superior sound control

Mineral wool's dense, rigid structure absorbs more mid-frequency sound than fiberglass. It's widely used in apartment buildings, hotels, recording studios, and any multi-tenant project where party wall STC ratings are specified.

03

Water-resistant — won't retain moisture

Mineral wool fibers are hydrophobic — they repel water rather than absorbing it. In crawl spaces, below-grade walls, and high-humidity environments, mineral wool maintains its R-value even when exposed to moisture that would degrade fiberglass.

Mineral Wool FAQ

Common questions about mineral wool.

What's the difference between mineral wool and fiberglass?

Both insulate effectively, but mineral wool is non-combustible, denser, more moisture-resistant, and better at sound absorption. Fiberglass is lighter and typically less per bag. The right choice depends on your application. See full comparison →

Is mineral wool itchy to install?

Less so than traditional fiberglass. Modern mineral wool batts have a slightly rougher feel, but most installers find them easier to handle than older fiberglass products. Long sleeves and gloves are still recommended, as with any insulation.

What R-values do you stock in mineral wool?

We stock R-13 (2×4 walls), R-21 (2×6 walls), and R-38 (attic and high-performance assemblies), in that order of most common use. Other R-values may be available on order — call to confirm availability in your area.
