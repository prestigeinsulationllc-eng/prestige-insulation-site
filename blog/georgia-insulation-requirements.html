Georgia Insulation Requirements: A Climate Zone Guide

Georgia spans three IECC climate zones — from hot-humid south Georgia to the mixed-humid mountains near the Tennessee and North Carolina borders. Requirements shift meaningfully depending on where in the state a job sits. Here's the breakdown by zone.

Georgia's climate zones

ZoneCities / RegionDescription
2AValdosta, Thomasville, far south GeorgiaHot-humid
3AAtlanta, Savannah, Macon, Augusta, Columbus — most of the stateMixed-humid
4ABlue Ridge, Dahlonega, far north Georgia mountainsMixed-humid (cooler)

Georgia adopts code on a delay — always confirm locally

Georgia's statewide energy code adoption has historically lagged the national IECC cycle, and some jurisdictions layer their own amendments on top. The values above reflect typical, widely-used specs for each zone, but the exact minimum enforced on a given permit depends on which code edition your local building department has adopted and when. Always confirm the current requirement with the county or city building department before finalizing a spec — this is standard practice regardless of state, but matters more in Georgia given adoption timing varies more than in some neighboring states.

What we hear most from Georgia contractors

  • Metro Atlanta new construction is almost entirely zone 3A specs — R-15 or R-13+3.75ci walls and R-38 attics are the norm on current permits.
  • Coastal Georgia (Savannah, Brunswick) runs hot-humid similar to zone 2A in practice, even though it's technically mapped 3A — mineral wool is a common upgrade near the coast for moisture resistance.
  • North Georgia mountain builds see the highest R-value requirements in the state and are the one area where basement wall insulation is commonly required.
