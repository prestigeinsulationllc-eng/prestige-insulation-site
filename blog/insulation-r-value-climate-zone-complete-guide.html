Insulation R-Value by Climate Zone: Complete 2026 Contractor Reference

What R-value does code require? This reference covers IECC climate zones 1 through 8 — walls, attics, floors, crawl spaces, and the products that meet each requirement.

IECC Climate Zone Map: Which Zone Are You In?

The International Energy Conservation Code (IECC) divides the US into 8 climate zones. Your zone determines the minimum R-value required for each building assembly.

  • Zone 1: South Florida, Hawaii — Hot-Humid
  • Zone 2: Gulf Coast, Phoenix AZ, Houston TX — Hot-Humid/Dry
  • Zone 3: Atlanta GA, Dallas TX, Los Angeles CA — Warm
  • Zone 4: Washington DC, Kansas City MO, Seattle WA — Mixed
  • Zone 5: Chicago IL, Denver CO, Indianapolis IN — Cold
  • Zone 6: Minneapolis MN, Boston MA, Portland ME — Cold
  • Zone 7: Northern Minnesota, Northern Montana — Very Cold
  • Zone 8: Alaska — Subarctic

Key Code Notes for Contractors

  • IECC 2021 wall change: Zones 4+ now require R-15 minimum for 2×4 exterior walls. R-13 is no longer code-minimum in zone 4. Many states have adopted IECC 2021 — confirm with your AHJ.
  • High-density batts: R-15 HD and R-21 HD are the same physical thickness as R-13 and R-19 respectively, but denser. They fit the same cavity and are the most common way to meet zone 4+ requirements without changing framing.
  • Local amendments: California (Title 24), Washington State, and several other states have adopted energy codes stricter than IECC. Always confirm with the local AHJ.
  • Thermal bridging: Stud framing reduces effective R-value by 15–20%. Some codes require continuous insulation to address this. Check your jurisdiction's requirements for CI specs.
