Insulation R-Value by Climate Zone: The Contractor's Reference

R-value requirements by IECC climate zone for walls, attics, floors, and basements. Updated for IECC 2021. A complete contractor reference guide.

Published July 3, 2026 · Prestige Insulation

One of the most common questions we get: "What R-value do I need for this job?" The answer depends entirely on your climate zone and the assembly type. This is the reference we wish existed when we started — zone-by-zone, assembly-by-assembly.

How climate zones work

The IECC (International Energy Conservation Code) divides the U.S. into 8 climate zones based on heating and cooling degree days. Zone 1 is the hottest (Miami), Zone 8 is the coldest (Fairbanks). Your local building department enforces the zone-specific requirements for your jurisdiction.

For below-grade applications, mineral wool (R-15) is the preferred product — it's non-combustible, moisture-resistant, and handles the humidity conditions common in basement assemblies. See our mineral wool page →

Quick reference: what to order by zone

  • Zone 1–2: R-13 walls · R-30 attic · R-13 floor
  • Zone 3: R-15 HD walls (2×4) · R-21 (2×6) · R-38 attic · R-19 floor
  • Zone 4: R-15 HD or 2×6 R-21 · R-38–R-49 attic · R-19–R-30 floor
  • Zone 5: 2×6 R-21 + R-3.8 ci · R-49 attic · R-30 floor
  • Zone 6–8: 2×6 R-21 + R-5–10 ci · R-49–R-60 attic · R-30 floor
