How to Calculate Insulation Bags for Any Job

The formula is straightforward: square footage divided by coverage per bag, plus 10% waste. Here's the complete reference with coverage charts for every R-value.

The Basic Formula

Bags = (Square footage ÷ Coverage per bag) × 1.10

The 1.10 factor adds 10% for cuts, waste, and the occasional damaged bag. Most contractors order exactly this number — enough to cover the job without excess.

Step 1: Measure Your Square Footage

For Walls

Measure the total linear footage of exterior walls, multiply by ceiling height, then subtract openings (windows and doors):

(Linear feet × Height) - (Window area + Door area) = Net wall sq ft

Example: 150 LF of exterior wall × 9' ceilings = 1,350 sq ft, minus 200 sq ft of windows/doors = 1,150 sq ft net.

For Attics

Measure the floor area of the attic (same as the conditioned floor area below). For a 2,000 sq ft house, the attic floor is approximately 2,000 sq ft.

For Floors Over Unconditioned Spaces

Measure the floor area directly above the unconditioned crawl space or garage.

Step 3: Run the Calculation

Example: 2,400 sq ft house, zone 5, 16" OC framing

  • Exterior walls (R-21): 1,200 net sq ft ÷ 28 sq ft/bag = 42.8 → 43 bags × 1.10 = 48 bags
  • Attic (R-49): 2,400 sq ft ÷ 14 sq ft/bag = 171.4 → 172 bags × 1.10 = 190 bags

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Ordering by gross wall area without subtracting windows and doors — you'll end up with 10–15% too much on a typical residential project
  • Using the wrong batt width — 16" OC framing uses 15" batts, 24" OC uses 23" batts. Ordering the wrong width means wasted product and re-cutting every batt
  • Forgetting the waste factor — 10% is standard. On a 200-bag order that's 20 bags — enough to matter if the job runs short
  • Not accounting for multiple R-values — most projects use different products in different areas. Calculate each assembly separately
